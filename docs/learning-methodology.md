# Personal Learning Methodology

A methodology tuned to one specific learner. It is not tied to one subject. English is the first topic; later topics (for example Chinese) reuse the same foundations and add their own topic plan on top.

> **In one sentence:** The learner is driven by curiosity and by concrete gaps, learns in short blocks, and keeps going when they *feel more capable*.

## Quick path

1. Do one **daily minimum** block (20–25 min) at **6:00**, before work. The material is real material from the learner's own goals.
2. In the **post-lunch slump**, do a **10-min low-effort block** (spaced review plus reading the news in the target language).
3. When confused, apply the **stuck protocol**. Never escape to news or social media.
4. Once a month, run the **mirror**: compare before and after evidence.

## 1. Learner profile

| Trait | Observation | Design consequence |
|-------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Motivation engine** | Curiosity and narrative: history, science, computing, stories. A generalist. Drew a lot as a kid. | Wrap practice in stories, puzzles, and real topics the learner cares about. |
| **Gap-driven** | Learns intensely while there is a concrete gap between "what I want to do" and "what I can do". Stops once the gap closes ("good enough"). | Always keep a **concrete, visible gap**. Use the target material itself as the study material. |
| **Focus span** | About 20–30 min on mechanical tasks. 1–2 h of hyperfocus when interested. | Base unit is a **25-min block**. Hyperfocus is a bonus, never the plan. |
| **Main breaking point** | *Not understanding* → discomfort → escape to novelty (news, social media). Boredom and curiosity jumps are secondary triggers. | A **stuck protocol** that is one step away. Turn the news habit into target-language input. |
| **All-or-nothing** | A passion can eat a whole day, including remote work, which is harmful. Then the learner drops it. | A **small daily minimum** plus a **weekday ceiling** and a **parking list**. |
| **Progress signal** | "I now understand or do naturally what I couldn't before." Streaks and points don't matter. | Make competence **visible** with before/after evidence. No streak gamification. |
| **Past tool lesson** | Duolingo (2019, 6–7 months): liked short units, mobile, and challenges. Quit because there was no real-world improvement. | Keep the short, mobile, challenge-based format, but on **real material**. |

### Energy map

| Time | Energy | Use it for |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 06:00 – noon | 🔋 Peak (wakes at 6:00; work also happens here) | **Main block**: the hardest cognitive work (new grammar, reading, writing). It comes *before* work. |
| After lunch | 🪫 Slump, peak procrastination, sometimes a nap | **Low-effort block**: spaced review plus news in the target language. |
| Evening / gym (3–4×/week) | Slower, recovering | **Listening** (podcasts) during the gym. Optional 5-min review before sleep (23:00). |
| Weekends | Free, social, gaming, sleep | Continue **parked** topics if motivated. Nothing is mandatory. |

## 2. Evidence-based principles

| Principle | What it means here | Source |
|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| **Retrieval practice** | Recalling without looking beats re-reading. | Roediger & Karpicke (2006) |
| **Spacing** | A little, often, beats a lot at once. | Cepeda et al. (2006); FSRS scheduling |
| **Focused feedback** | Correcting 2–3 targeted errors beats marking everything. | Sheen & Ellis (2008); Ellis et al. (2008) |
| **Explicit grammar instruction** | Explicit rule teaching is effective and durable for adults. | Norris & Ortega (2000) |
| **Input volume** | Words need ~10+ encounters in context to stick. Reading must be frequent. | Nation (2001, 2014) |
| **Generation effect** | Producing an answer or fix yourself (with a hint) beats being told. | Slamecka & Graf (1978) |
| **Curiosity gap** | A question you want answered primes attention and memory. | Loewenstein (1994); Gruber et al. (2014) |
| **No "learning styles"** | Matching instruction to a VARK style does not improve outcomes. Preferences matter only for adherence. | Pashler et al. (2008) |

## 3. The 7 principles

1. **The material is the goal.** Learn with what you actually want to use (real articles, books, your own posts), not generic drills.
2. **Always a visible gap.** Use a concrete, near target ("read this article without a translator"), never "improve my English".
3. **Small daily minimum at 06:00.** 20–25 min before work. Hard things go where the energy is.
4. **Ceiling plus parking list.** On weekdays, hyperfocus topics are written down and resumed later. No marathons that eat work.
5. **Stuck protocol.** Confusion triggers one fixed, short action, never an escape.
6. **Produce, don't only consume.** Write and publish. Use what was learned.
7. **Visible progress by comparison.** Before/after evidence instead of streaks.

---

## 4. Daily rhythm

| Block | Duration | Mandatory | Content |
|-------|----------|-----------|---------|
| Main (06:00) | 20–25 min | **Yes** | Hardest item of the day: new unit, reading, writing. |
| Minimum variant | 5 min | Fallback on bad days | One tiny item (a few reviews or one exercise set). **Never zero.** |
| Slump (after lunch) | ~10 min | No | Spaced review + news in the target language. |
| Gym | Any | No | Podcasts on favourite topics (history, science, tech). |
| Before sleep | ~5 min | No | Light review. |

Sessions **end automatically** at the planned time. Stopping mid-way, on a cliffhanger, makes returning easier (the "Hemingway trick").

---

## 5. Stuck protocol

When something is not understood:

1. Press **"I don't get it"**. You get a short explanation in Spanish, from a *different angle*: an analogy (often from programming) or a contrast with Spanish.
2. Max ~2 minutes, then return to the **exact same spot**.
3. If it is still unclear, mark it as a **doubt** and continue. Doubts are reviewed later.
4. Never leave the material to "check something quickly".

## 6. Parking list

- On weekdays, when a topic triggers hyperfocus outside the plan, write one line in the parking list and close it.
- Parked items are offered after work or on weekends.
- Nothing is lost; it is only deferred.

## 7. Monthly mirror

Once a month, collect evidence of competence:

- [ ] Re-read a text from the first weeks. Count the unknown words then vs. now.
- [ ] Put the first post next to the latest one (scores, error types, naturalness).
- [ ] Write new "now I can…" statements in the can-do log.
- [ ] Review the most frequent error types and check whether they are decreasing.

---

## 8. Reuse for future topics

For a new topic (for example Chinese), keep sections 1–7 and add a topic plan that defines:

| Item | Question to answer |
|------|-------------------|
| Real goal material | What does the learner actually want to read, do, or produce? |
| Visible gap | What is the next concrete thing they can't do yet? |
| Explicit knowledge source | The equivalent of the grammar book: structured units with exercises. |
| Daily input | The equivalent of the daily article. |
| Production task | The equivalent of posts. |
| Mirror evidence | What before/after artifact proves progress? |
